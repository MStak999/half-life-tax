The Economic Anatomy of a Dangerous Autonomous Agent

An agent that poses existential-level risk needs compute to operate. Compute costs money. Someone has to pay. This calculator traces the economic chain that would need to hold for a dangerous autonomous agent to actually run, and shows what breaks at each link. Companion to the Half-Life Tax model.

Can a Rogue Agent Sustain Itself?

A self-sustaining agent must generate revenue exceeding its own inference cost. It must complete tasks for paying customers, but with no quality assurance. How does it compete?

Min price = break-even compute cost per successful output. Supervised agent includes verification. The rogue agent must charge more than its min price, but less than the alternatives, while delivering failures 2 out of 3 times.
